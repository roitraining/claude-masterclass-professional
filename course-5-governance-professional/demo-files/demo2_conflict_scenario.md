# Demo 2 Sample: A Conflict of Authority

## The situation

It is the second week of the quarter. Marcus Oyelaran (CRO) wants to give all 90 Sales pilot seats a connection to the customer relationship system so Claude can draft renewal proposals from live account records. He has told his team it starts Monday. A large renewal with Halvern Hospital Network closes in five weeks.

Three leaders disagree:

- **Marcus Oyelaran, CRO:** "Every day of delay costs us deals. Our competitors are not waiting for a committee. I own the revenue number and I accept the risk."
- **Priya Raman, General Counsel:** "Halvern's contract requires 30 days' written notice before its data goes into new automated processing. The account records hold Halvern data. Nothing starts until notice is given and I have reviewed it."
- **Tomas Lindqvist, CISO:** "Account records include contact details and order notes. This is a Tier 3 use. The controls for it are not in place for Sales groups yet. I will not approve it."

Ben Adeyemi (COO) chairs the AI Governance Board. Dana Whitfield (CEO) has said she wants speed and no surprises.

## Facts everyone agrees on

1. The connection would expose account records for all of Sales' customers, not only Halvern.
2. The Halvern clause is in a signed contract.
3. The current policy draft says "management approval" and does not say whose.
4. No written risk acceptance exists for any AI use.

## Questions for the class

1. What is each leader's authority, and where does it end?
2. Which of the three positions is a preference and which is an obligation?
3. Who may accept a Tier 3 risk? May Marcus accept it for himself?
4. What does the decision look like if it is not a plain yes or no?

## Resolution pattern (reveal after the class has tried)

1. **Separate obligations from preferences.** A contract clause is an obligation that nobody inside the company may waive. Speed and a block on tools are preferences that can be traded.
2. **Say who may accept what.** A person who benefits from the use does not accept its risk alone. Tier 3 risk acceptance is written, signed by the accountable executive, and recorded.
3. **Escalate to a named tie-breaker with a deadline.** The Governance Board chair decides within 3 business days. If the chair is a party, the CEO decides.
4. **Look for a conditional decision.** Approve a narrower version now: exclude Halvern records, start with Tier 2 data, and add Tier 3 data when the controls and the notice are done.
5. **Record it.** The decision, its conditions, who accepted the risk, and the date go in the Board minutes.
